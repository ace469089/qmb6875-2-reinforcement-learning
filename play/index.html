Impression 1 of 50

Five ads, each with a secret click rate. Every turn you show one ad and see if it got a click. The robot plays the same 50 turns with Thompson sampling: it keeps a belief about each ad and shows the one whose lucky draw is highest. Most clicks wins.

Why does the robot explore at all?

Exploit = show what has looked best so far. Explore = try another ad in case your estimate is wrong. Never exploring can lock you onto a bad ad after one lucky click. Thompson sampling explores each ad in proportion to the chance it's actually the best.

In the repo's 10,000-impression test with 2–5% click rates, Thompson lost 47.9 clicks to the perfect strategy, a classic A/B test lost 62.4, and never exploring lost 117.9.

Watch Q-learning learn the offer rule

Episode 0

Offers arrive one at a time (20 possible levels). The robot can accept (paid the offer) or reject (wait a period, worth 10% less). It keeps a report card Q(offer, action) and nudges it after every move. It is never told offers are uniform.

Q(s,a) ← Q(s,a) + α · [ reward + 0.9 · max Q(next) − Q(s,a) ]

What to try

Drag α down to 0.01 and ε to 0.05: learning crawls, because "reject" is rarely tried at high offers. Push α to 0.5: it learns fast but jitters. Tick "decaying α" for the steady version.

Switch to SARSA: it settles lower on purpose, near the value of a robot that keeps exploring (the dashed line). That's on-policy vs. off-policy learning.
